# Project: Brightpane Window Co. (FICTIONAL EXAMPLE)

Everything in this file is made up to show how a filled-in CONTEXT looks. The company, town, people, prices and reviews do not exist. Do not send messages using these facts.

## 1. The business
- **Name and sender:** Brightpane Window Co., written by Jamie (owner).
- **What you sell, in one plain sentence:** We clean windows for shops, offices and homes in Maple Falls and the towns around it.
- **Who buys it:** Shop owners and office managers on Main St and in the business parks; homeowners in two-story houses. The owner or office manager decides alone for anything under a few hundred dollars.
- **Postal address for cold email footers:** Brightpane Window Co., PO Box 000, Maple Falls (fictional).
- **Languages you can actually serve in:** English. Spanish on the phone with Luis, the crew lead. Written quotes are English only.

## 2. The outcome (what they get)
- A storefront that looks open and cared for to everyone walking by.
- Clean glass on a schedule, without having to remember to book it.
- A crew that shows up when it says it will.

## 3. The gaps you look for
| Gap | How to verify it for one prospect | How to say it in owner words |
|---|---|---|
| Streaked or dusty storefront glass | Their own Google Maps or social photos from the last 60 days | "the front glass in your photos from last month" |
| Hard-to-reach upper windows | Street view shows a second story with no visible cleaning access | "the upstairs windows people see from across the street" |
| Moving into a new space | Their own post or a public lease announcement | "with the move in March" |

## 4. Offer, prices and terms
- **Price:** first clean $149 for a standard storefront (up to 10 panes, inside and out). After that, from $99 a visit. Homes quoted on a call.
- **Term:** no contract. Book monthly, every other month, or one time. Cancel any time.
- **Risk reversal / guarantee:** if you're not happy with a clean, tell us within 48 hours and we come back and redo it free.
- **What's NOT included:** screens, tracks, and anything above the second story.
- **Price in cold email?** Yes, the first-clean price only.

## 5. Live offers and real deadlines
| Offer | Who qualifies | Ends |
|---|---|---|
| First clean $149 instead of $179 | New commercial customers in Maple Falls | 2099-12-31 (example date) |

## 6. Allowed proof
- Insured with general liability coverage; certificate on request.
- Every crew member is background-checked.
- 4.8 stars from 52 reviews on Google (example figure).
- **Client stories allowed in cold email?** No. Only on the website and on calls, and only with the client's permission.

## 7. Red lines for this project
- Never promise a specific date before checking the schedule.
- Never claim "eco-friendly" or "streak-free guaranteed".
- No texts unless the prospect texted first.
- Never mention other clients by name.

## 8. Voice
- **Sounds like:** a friendly local tradesperson who notices things. Plain words, short sentences.
- **Words to use:** glass, storefront, the front windows, upstairs windows, crew, visit.
- **Words to avoid:** "solutions", "premium", "pristine", "sparkling", "transform".
- **Sign-off:** "Jamie, Brightpane Window Co."

## 9. CTA by stage
| Stage | CTA |
|---|---|
| Cold | "Want me to put you down for a first clean?" (reply-based, no link) |
| Warm | "Pick any morning that works here: {booking link}" |
| Hot | "I can have the crew there Tuesday at 7, before you open. Should I book it?" |
| Win-back | One note after 90 days: "Want us back on the schedule this spring?" Then stop. |

## 10. Copy rules
- Cold email order: outcome, the gap we saw, what changes for them, first-clean price, the redo guarantee, one open question, CTA.
- Max 80 words in a first-touch cold email body.
- Subject lines: lower case, under 40 characters, name the street or the storefront, never "Re:".
- Never compliment the business in the first line.
