# Halbert module: direct-response copy craft (rule-filtered)

Ideas from Gary Halbert's letters (The Boron Letters, 1984, and issues of The Gary Halbert Letter, published free by the Halbert estate), plus public summaries by other copywriters. All restated in our own words. No letter, chapter or ad is reproduced. Sources: SOURCES.md.

**Precedence.** The red lines in PLAYBOOK.md section 7, LESSONS.md, your project's rules (projects/<project>/CONTEXT.md, optional COPY-RULES.md), your measured outcomes and the graded rules in RESEARCH-EVIDENCE.md all win over this file. It is an idea source, not a license to change copy that works. Additive only.

**Evidence grade: D.** Practitioner opinion. His numbers are self-reported and sometimes disagree between his own issues. If an idea matches a graded rule, cite the rule. If not, it is a test idea (section 6).

**Identity.** Never imitate or claim to be Gary Halbert. Never name him to a prospect, copy his persona (profanity, shock, bragging) or borrow his results.

**Tags**, judged against the red lines in PLAYBOOK section 7 and your project's rules: **[OK]** use as is. **[ADAPT]** use the safe version. **[TEST]** only as an A/B arm. **[BANNED]** never.

## 1. Market and opening

- **Starving crowd beats a better product.** [OK] Copy points existing desire at an offer; it can't create desire. Reach people already feeling the pain first.
- **Rank lists by recency, then frequency, then spend.** [OK] Your own happy customers beat any cold list.
- **Watch what people buy, not what they say.** [OK] Decide from clicks and replies, and use customers' own words.
- **What keeps running is working.** [OK] Copy the appeal of long-running ads, never the words.
- **Moments of change** (a move, a new season, a trial ending) open people to offers. [OK] Verified events only.
- **Tailor per segment.** [OK] Naming the reader's trade or town beats "occupant". Verified data only.
- **Look personal, don't hide.** [ADAPT] Real name in From, plain text, a subject that reads like a note. CAN-SPAM: accurate From and header, non-deceptive subject, notice that it's commercial, postal address, working opt-out. No fake Re:/Fwd:. On social, disclose that you sell the thing.
- **Delivery first.** [OK] Sender reputation and inbox placement, measured, not assumed.
- **Delay the "Oh yuck".** [OK] Let the reader meet the person and the value before the sales machinery. Full terms always show before anyone pays.

## 2. Headlines, openers and voice

- **Headline is most of the result.** [OK] News plus a specific benefit; timeframe, place or job help. Online, the page title and search snippet are the headline. Practical "how to" titles beat clever ones; retitle and test one variable at a time (SUBJECT-LINES.md).
- **Grabbers must fit.** [ADAPT] An unrelated trick breeds resentment. Never fake alerts, invoices or account notices.
- **Openers.** [ADAPT] A desire question, a sourced statistic, the reader's place, a belief-breaking claim, an honest limit. All true. Question vs statement is a test.
- **Story.** [ADAPT] One true short story carries the reader. True only.
- **Name in the subject** was a favorite of his; follow SUBJECT-LINES.md and RESEARCH-EVIDENCE.md instead.
- **Friend recommending something.** [OK] Everyday words, contractions, questions you then answer. No "solutions" or "leverage". Sell, don't perform: cut lines that only sound smart.
- **Editorial look.** [ADAPT] Teach something true, mention the product once and late. Never a fake article or review.

## 3. Clarity and editing

- **Short everything, white space, explain everything.** [OK] Writers overestimate readers. Past a point, more information stops helping.
- **Read aloud** and rewrite every stumble. [OK] Mandatory before returning copy.
- **Keep the reader moving.** [ADAPT] Each line pulls into the next; follow your project's punctuation rules.
- **Edit in passes** (idea from Bond Halbert's editing book): does line 1 earn line 2; clarity; a reason to keep reading in every paragraph; punch (concrete nouns, the reader's words, cut "that", "just", "really"). [ADAPT]

## 4. Proof, offer, close

- **Specific beats general.** [ADAPT] Exact sourced figures; never invent a decimal.
- **Interesting fact, then bridge to the offer.** [OK] True, sourced, relevant.
- **Bullets tease a true benefit.** [ADAPT] Reframe a dull fact so it intrigues; never distort. True for every reader.
- **Belief.** [ADAPT] People don't buy because they don't want it, can't afford it, or don't believe you. Fix belief with a named contact, real public address, checkable facts. Testimonials only if real, permissioned and allowed by your project.
- **Reason why.** [ADAPT] A deal without a reason gets doubted. Best reason: the reader's own verified situation.
- **Offer before words.** [OK] A strong offer plainly written beats a weak one beautifully written. When a test loses, question segment and offer before rewording. Only terms in your project's CONTEXT.md.
- **Big idea first.** [ADAPT] Most creative time goes to the angle. It must be true. No celebrity tie-ins, stunts or contests.
- **Price is relative.** [ADAPT] Anchors shape perception. Never vary price by a protected characteristic.
- **Two steps beat one.** [OK] The cold ask is a reply or a small free step. Example: "Want me to send the checklist?"
- **Guarantees.** [TEST] Only guarantees your project really offers; changes are the owner's call; never built on customers forgetting. Fictional example: "First clean $149. Not happy, we redo it free."
- **Close.** [ADAPT] Ask directly, give every step and the terms beside the button. Cost of waiting and deadlines must be real.
- **P.S.** [TEST in email, OK on pages] Restate one benefit; any urgency must be real.
- **Long-form order** (pages, not cold email): facts, benefits, strongest true offer, headline, who is writing, what the reader gains, facts list, true reason for the deal, one action, P.S. AIDA works too: desire means painting the concrete after-picture, never results you haven't recorded. [ADAPT]

## 5. Process, testing, mindset

- **Saturate**: product facts (including negatives), customer words, competitor ads, past winners. Numbered notes, star the best, step away, then draft. [OK]
- **Re-read proven winners** before writing. Great copy looks spontaneous but is planned. [OK]
- **Draft fast, edit later**; start with the easy parts. [OK]
- **Test the idea first** with your best segment and copy; nobody reliably guesses winners. Small test, bigger test, rollout, using the stats floor in RESEARCH-EVIDENCE.md. A winner on your best names may fail on the long tail, so re-check. Keep winners until numbers drop. [OK]
- **Follow-ups** restate the outcome in one line plus one new verified fact. [ADAPT] Cadence from RESEARCH-EVIDENCE.md. His recorded-call follow-ups are [BANNED] (TCPA).
- **After the order**: confirm, restate the first win, one next action. [OK]
- **HALT**: never reply to an angry prospect while hungry, angry, lonely or tired. [OK]
- **Write as if this one message must work.** Pressure on the writer, never the reader. [OK]

## 6. Conflicts and test ideas

**Banned:** fake urgency stamps or deadlines; fake scarcity; invented stories or reasons; brand look-alikes; earnings headlines (unless your project allows a truthful version); recorded voice follow-ups; contests as hype; exclamation marks and ALL CAPS in email; shock, profanity, insults; refund-prevention tricks; fear selling without sourced facts about the reader's real situation.
**Adapt:** personal style without disguise; real permissioned proof only; real numbers only; your real public address; "free" and bargains only within your project's hype rules.
**Superseded or deferred:** prospect name in subject (evidence says no); long copy (cold email stays short, long copy only below the fold on pages); physical mailers (budget, per project).

**Test ideas (grade D, need RESEARCH-EVIDENCE.md stats before becoming rules):** plain text from a named person vs HTML card; offer line with a true reason vs bare; P.S. vs none; page section built from ranked spoken lines vs current; long AIDA block below the fold vs current; "how to" subject vs current; true-story opener vs pain question; follow-up restating the outcome plus a new fact vs current.

**Case lesson (structure only):** his best-known mailing was a short, personal-looking letter about the reader's own family name. Transferable: field research first, a reason why about the reader, a personal look, a small first yes leading to a bigger sale. Reported results disagree, so never cite them. Its implied scarcity and persona are BANNED for us. Personalize only with verified data, never merge tags alone.
