# Funnel stages: what to say, and what to hold back, at each temperature (Martha)

Why this exists. Wording has to be different at each part of the funnel. A fact that helps at one stage can hurt at another. Example rule: a cold email offering a free trial says the trial is free and stops there; the reader learns the billing details (no card needed, how to cancel) later, at the button and before any charge. Another: use the reader's own word for the problem. If they call it a "scorecard" or a "callback list", say that, not your internal term.

Scope: every project, every outbound or funnel asset. Precedence: PLAYBOOK red lines and LESSONS win, then the project's own rules in projects/<project>/CONTEXT.md (offer, prices, CTAs, your rulings), then this file. Read it with SUBJECT-LINES.md for any email.

**Step zero on every task: name the reader's stage before writing a word.** "No card needed" answers a worry a cold reader doesn't have yet. It plants it.

## 1. The four stages

| Stage | Who | What they're thinking | Our one job |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Cold** | Never heard of you. Cold email, cold DM, first ad view. | "Who is this? Is it about me?" | Earn the next line, then a tiny yes (reply or click). |
| **Warm** | Clicked, visited, replied, asked for the link, signed up for a reminder. | "Is this real? What's the catch?" | Remove the catch. Get the start. |
| **Hot** | Trial started, in the product, booked, on the call, at checkout. | "Is it working for me? What will I pay, when?" | First win fast, then full honest terms. |
| **Win-back** | Trial ended unpaid, cancelled, no-show, went quiet after starting. | "Was it worth it? Did I miss something?" | One reason to come back, one clean door, then stop. |

## 2. What to reveal when (the core rule)

| Information | Cold | Warm | Hot | Win-back |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| The outcome in their words | Yes, line 1 | Yes, headline | Yes, as their first win | Yes, what stopped working |
| One proof fact | One checkable fact max (about them, or a true founder line) | Ratings, real counts, screenshots, FAQ (only proof allowed by CONTEXT.md) | Their own results | What they had while it ran |
| The offer | The project's cold offer line (from CONTEXT.md) | Same, plus the reassurance at the button | Exact terms | Plain price to come back |
| Billing mechanics (card, auto-renew, cancel steps, trial-end behaviour) | **Never** | Only a one-line reassurance near the button ("no card needed") | **Required**: price, when billing starts, how to cancel, before any card is taken | Price and cancel line |
| Price figure | Per project (see below) | Pricing section, below the outcome | Yes, exact | Yes |
| Feature list | No. One outcome, one mechanism at most | 3 to 6 short benefits | Step-by-step setup | The one feature they used |
| CTA | Interest: reply, "worth a look?", one link | One button: start / get the link / book | One action | Reply or one link, then stop |
| Length | 25 to 100 words (cap 110) | A page; scannable | Short steps, one per email | Under 80 words |

**Price at the cold stage is a per-project decision**, recorded in projects/<project>/CONTEXT.md:
- Self-serve product with a free trial: usually hold the price. It is a checkout fact, and the cold job is the tiny yes.
- Done-for-you service sold on a call, to buyers whose top distrust is long contracts or surprise bills: the price and the no-commitment term can belong in the cold email, because they are the risk reversal. Fictional example (Brightpane Window Co.): "$149 first clean, then from $99 a visit, cancel anytime." What still stays out of cold: payment mechanics (how and when billing runs, agreement terms), long client stories, tool words.

**Why billing mechanics never go cold.** Common practice puts "no credit card required" at the signup button and on the page a visitor lands on, not in outreach. In a cold email the line answers a question the stranger hasn't asked, adds words, and turns the email into a billing conversation. The no-card fact is a warm-stage reassurance; the reader sees it when they click.

**Why full terms are required hot.** When a card is taken for a trial, the price, the date billing starts and how to cancel must be shown at that moment. Visa's subscription rules (in force since April 2020) require express consent at enrolment, a copy of the terms, and a reminder with a cancel link at least 7 days before the first charge when a trial ends. The FTC's negative-option rules point the same way. Holding billing details back at cold is only honest because they are shown in full before anyone can be charged.

**Honesty test for "free".** A cold line like "try it free for {n} days" is true only if a reader who starts and does nothing is never charged. Check the project's real trial behaviour in CONTEXT.md. If that behaviour ever changes, the cold line changes the same day.

## 3. CTA ladder

1. **Cold first touch:** an interest question or a reply ask ("Worth a look?", "Is this on your radar?", "Reply and tell me what happened"). Gong Labs, 304k emails: on a first touch an interest CTA did about twice as well as asking for a specific time; the specific ask works better once interest exists.
2. **Cold follow-up:** one new fact, the same soft CTA. Exit by touch 3 or 4.
3. **Warm:** one action button with the reassurance under it (for a trial: "Start free" + "no card needed"; for a service: "Book a quote" + the no-commitment line).
4. **Hot:** one action per email (finish setup, approve the first job), then the trial-end or decision email with the terms and one action.
5. **Win-back:** one door (reply or link), say it is the last email, then stop.

Service version (call-based sales): cold = interest question, or the project's live offer with a short call as the CTA if CONTEXT.md says so. Warm (replied or clicked) = the booking link plus 1 to 3 questions. Hot (booked or on the call) = recap their problem, exact price, guarantee wording, what happens next, decision. Win-back = one check-back on a date they choose.

## 4. What well-known companies do (summary of public practice)

These lines summarize widely documented public practice, in our own words. Verify a detail on the company's own page before quoting it to anyone.

| Company | Cold / top of funnel | Warm (landing, signup) | Hot (trial, in product, checkout) | Held back until later | Replicate |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Gong** | Research-led content and short, interest-CTA outbound | Demo request, proof from data | Sales-led onboarding | Price (quote only, on a call) | Interest CTA on first touch; data as proof |
| **HubSpot** | Free tools and content; outbound asks about a problem, not the product | "Get started free", "no credit card required" next to the button | In-app checklist, upgrade prompts tied to limits | Paid tier prices until the pricing page | Free thing first, upgrade at the limit |
| **Calendly** | Product-led: every booking link is an ad | "Sign up free" | Onboarding emails, one setup step each | Paid features until in product | Each email = one step |
| **Loom** | Every shared video is the ad | Free signup | Upgrade prompts at use limits | Billing until the limit hits | Let the product show itself |
| **Slack** | Word of mouth, team invites | Free plan | Trial of paid tier inside the workspace; reminders before it ends | Paid price until the team uses it | Trial inside the product, reminder before the end |
| **Notion** | Templates and creators | Free signup | Upgrade at limits | Price on the pricing page only | Free value before any talk of money |
| **Superhuman** | Waitlist and scarcity of access | Survey, then onboarding call | 1:1 onboarding, first win in session | Price until the call or checkout | First win inside the first session |
| **Zapier** | Search pages for each "connect X to Y" job | Free plan, then paid trial | Trial emails show what you built; trial end shows the plan | Price on pricing page | Lead with the job they searched for |
| **Shopify** | "Start for free" ads | Trial signup, no card at start | Onboarding checklist, then plan choice and card before the store goes live | Card until they pick a plan | No card at start; card at the moment of value |
| **Canva** | Free product and templates | Free signup | Pro trial; clear reminder before billing | Pro price until trial | Clear reminder before billing |

General evidence:
- Opt-in trials (no card) are widely reported to convert to paid around 18% and opt-out (card up front) around 49%, but far more people start a no-card trial, so no-card can end with more paying customers overall (secondhand benchmark, Monetizely FAQ). Takeaway: the no-card start is a warm-stage lever, and the trial-end email (hot) carries the paid conversion.
- Cold email length: short wins (see COLD-OUTREACH.md and SUBJECT-LINES.md).
- Awareness ladder (Eugene Schwartz, Breakthrough Advertising, 1966): less aware readers get the problem and the outcome; solution-aware readers get the product; the most aware get the offer and terms. The four stages map onto it: cold = problem-aware, warm = solution/product-aware, hot = most aware.

## 5. Patterns to copy (rules)

1. Cold sells the outcome and a tiny yes. Never the billing mechanics.
2. "No card needed" lives at the button the warm reader is about to press.
3. Price and terms show up in full before a card is taken, never after.
4. One stage, one CTA. The CTA grows with trust: reply, then click, then start, then pay.
5. Proof grows with the stage: a founder line, then ratings and counts, then their own results.
6. Length grows warm and shrinks hot: short cold, full page warm, one step per email hot.
7. Onboarding emails each carry one action. The trial-end or decision email carries the terms.
8. Win-back makes one offer, says it is the last, then stops.
9. Don't repeat a lower stage's job to a higher-stage reader. A trialist doesn't need the cold pitch; they need their next step.
10. Every stage's words must stay true for every reader that stage reaches. If a claim depends on a segment (for example, returning customers don't get the intro price), keep it out of shared copy or gate it.

## 6. Templates by stage

Slots come from projects/<project>/CONTEXT.md: `{outcome}`, `{offer_line}`, `{trial_line}`, `{cta}`, `{allowed_proof}`.

**Cold first touch.** Line 1: the pain or outcome in their words (a question they'd answer "yes" to). Line 2: a true founder fact or what it does for them. Line 3: one checkable fact. Line 4: `{offer_line}` (for a self-serve trial, the free-trial line with no billing words). Line 5: one interest ask. Sign-off: first name of the real sender.
**Cold follow-up.** One new fact or the setup in one sentence, the offer line, one soft question. No card, no "nothing to cancel".
**Warm page / signup email.** Headline outcome. One button. Under it, the one-line reassurance. 3 benefits. One proof line that's true and allowed.
**Hot onboarding.** Subject = the step. One action. A help path (just reply).
**Hot trial end / decision.** Date it ends, what happens with and without a card (or with and without a yes), price, how to cancel, one button.
**Win-back.** What stopped, price to come back, reply or link, "last email".

Fictional example (Brightpane Window Co., commercial storefronts in Maple Falls):
- Cold: "Streaky glass after the spring pollen? We clean storefronts on Main Street before 8 am, so nothing blocks your door. $149 first clean, then from $99 a visit, cancel anytime. Worth a quote?"
- Warm (replied): booking link plus two questions (how many panes, how often).
- Hot (booked): recap, exact price for their panes, satisfaction guarantee wording from CONTEXT.md, the date of the first clean.
- Win-back (no-show): "Still want that first clean? Pick a week and I'll hold a morning slot. Last note from me."

## 7. Applying this to a project

For each project, record in projects/<project>/CONTEXT.md:
- The cold offer line and whether the price figure is a cold-stage fact (section 2).
- The exact trial or guarantee behaviour, so the honesty test can be run.
- The CTA for each stage (section 3).
- Which proof is allowed at which stage.
- Any words the reader uses for the problem, and any internal terms to avoid.

Then map each asset (each cold email, page, onboarding email, win-back) to one stage and check it against section 8.

## 8. Checklist (run before returning any funnel copy)

1. Stage named in the output block (`stage: cold|warm|hot|win-back`).
2. Nothing revealed that belongs to a later stage (cold: search the text for card, credit, billing, charge, cancel, auto-renew, subscription; and the price figure if the project holds it at cold).
3. Everything the stage requires is there (hot with a card: price, billing start date, how to cancel).
4. One CTA, matching the ladder.
5. Length inside the stage's band, counted.
6. The offer line is true for every reader this asset reaches.
