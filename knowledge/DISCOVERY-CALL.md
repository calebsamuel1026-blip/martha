# Discovery and close call: 15 minutes with a small-business owner

Built from SALES-FRAMEWORKS.md (Sandler, Gap Selling, SPIN, Voss, Challenger, Cialdini), the PLAYBOOK.md spine and VOICE-OF-CUSTOMER.md. Works for any project. Project facts (price, terms, guarantee, live offer, setup steps, honest limits) come from projects/<project>/CONTEXT.md and override anything here. Examples use the fictional Brightpane Window Co. or {{placeholders}}.

**Who runs it.** A human (the owner or their rep) speaks. Martha writes the prep sheet, the question order and the follow-up. Martha never places calls (TCPA: no AI voice calls without prior written consent; PLAYBOOK red line 4).

**What we sell, said the same way every time.** Before the call, copy the one- or two-sentence offer from the offer section of CONTEXT.md into the prep sheet and say it word for word. Add a live offer only if CONTEXT.md lists it, the prospect qualifies, and you can state its real end date. Anything outside your control (third-party approvals, timelines you don't own) is said as a limit, never a promise.

> Example (Brightpane): "First clean is $149. After that it's from $99 a visit, and you can cancel anytime."

---

## Before the call (5 minutes)

1. Read your research on the prospect. Note what you already know so you don't ask it (site, reviews, hours, languages, how long they've been in business).
2. Pick the one verified problem you'll name (e.g. "requests sent after 6pm get no reply until the next day").
3. Check CONTEXT.md: is an offer live, and does this prospect qualify? Write the exact end date.
4. Check which language they prefer. If Spanish, read SPANISH-SELLING.md and decide now whether the call runs in Spanish or in English with a written Spanish follow-up.
5. Leave the blanks in the gap line below empty. Their numbers go there, not yours.

---

## The call, minute by minute

### 0:00 to 1:00 Rapport and the up-front contract (Sandler)

> "Hi {name}, it's {your name} from {company}. Thanks for getting back to me. Is now still good?"
>
> "I've got about 15 minutes. I'll ask a few questions about how {the relevant work} gets handled. Then I'll tell you straight if I think we can help. If it's not a fit, just say so, that's a fine answer. If it is, we'll pick a start date. Sound fair?"

Wait for the yes. If they say "I only have 5 minutes", agree and cut to questions 2, 4 and 6.

### 1:00 to 8:00 Discovery (6 to 8 questions, one at a time)

Order follows SPIN: almost no situation questions, then problem, implication, need-payoff. After each answer, label or mirror once before the next question. Adapt the wording to the project's problem (from CONTEXT.md).

1. **Where work comes from (situation, only if unknown).** "Where do most of your customers come from right now?"
2. **Current state (problem).** "When you're busy and a new request comes in, what happens?"
3. **Size it (problem).** "Roughly how many requests a week? How many slip?"
4. **Value of a job (they know this best).** "What's a typical job or customer worth to you, ballpark?"
5. **Implication.** "When someone doesn't hear back, where do they go next?"
6. **Follow-up gap.** "After you send a price and they go quiet, what happens?"
7. **What's been tried (pain funnel).** "What have you tried for this before? How'd it go?"
8. **Need-payoff.** "If that was handled, what would it change for you?"

Two optional questions when time allows:
- "Who else weighs in on a decision like this?" (ask early if you hear "we")
- "What made this worth a call now?"

**Disqualify out loud** when they clearly don't have the problem or the volume (set the threshold per project in CONTEXT.md): "Honestly, at that volume I don't think you need us. If that changes, call me."

### 8:00 to 10:00 Quantify the gap in their own numbers (Gap Selling, Voss summary)

Use only what they said. Say the result as one plain sentence. They do the math with you, out loud, so the number is theirs.

> "Let me make sure I've got it. You said about {their number} requests slip in a busy week, and maybe {their share} of those would have become a job. A job runs about {their job value}. So even one a month that gets away is about {one job value} going to someone else. Is that right?"

Wait for "that's right." If they correct a number, use theirs. If they won't give numbers, use one: "Would it be fair to say even one a month slips?" and stop there.

Rules for this step:
- One comparison, not a chain. "One job a month" is enough.
- Never bring a number they didn't give, and never repeat a template number from an email as if it were a fact about them.
- "Could", not "will".

### 10:00 to 12:00 The offer (Hormozi value equation, said plainly)

Tie it to the gap they named, not a feature list. Outcome first, then what it costs, then what they do. See HORMOZI.md.

> "Here's what we'd do: {the outcome from CONTEXT.md, in one or two sentences}."
>
> "{Price and terms from CONTEXT.md, word for word, including any guarantee or risk reversal.}"
>
> (Only if live in CONTEXT.md and they qualify:) "And because {the real condition}, you get {real discount} until {real date}."
>
> "Your part is {their real effort, e.g. a short setup call}. We do the rest."

Honest limits to say before they ask: anything in the honest-limits section of CONTEXT.md (third-party approvals, things you can't promise like more jobs or revenue, language limits if relevant).

### 12:00 to 14:00 Objections (see map below), then the close

Close with a no-oriented or plain question (Voss, PLAYBOOK section 6):

> "Based on what you told me, this fits. Would it be a bad idea to get it started this week?"

or

> "Yes, no, or not now are all fine. Which is it?"

### 14:00 to 15:00 The commitment (a dated next step, every time)

If yes, ask for exactly what setup needs (from CONTEXT.md) and set dates while still on the call:

> "Great. I need {the setup items}. Can we do {the setup step} {day} at {time}? Then you'd be up and running by {date}."

If not now: "What date should I check back?" Write it down. If no date, close it cleanly: "No problem. I won't chase you."

Reinforce after a yes: what happens next, when, and who does what. Then record the outcome and their answers in your pipeline.

## Objection map

Method for every objection (PLAYBOOK section 6): pause, label or mirror, one calibrated question, then the honest answer, then confirm. Lines are starting points, not scripts. Fill terms from CONTEXT.md.

| Objection | What's usually underneath | First move (understand) | Honest answer (your terms) | Confirm / next |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Price: "That's a lot"** | Burned before, or can't tie it to a job | "Sounds like it's a lot next to what you spend now. What would it need to do to be worth it?" | "Fair. You said a job runs about {their number}. If this catches {a small number} a year, it's paid for." Then restate the risk reversal from CONTEXT.md (e.g. cancel anytime). | "What part of that doesn't sit right?" |
| **"I already have someone"** (family member, office manager, another vendor) | Loyalty, control, fear of a fight | "Good. What do they handle best?" then "What happens after hours or on a weekend?" | "We don't replace them. We cover {the gap}. They keep doing what they do." Never knock the current person. | "Would it make sense to include them in the setup?" |
| **"Send me info"** | Polite no, or real interest with no time | "Happy to. What would you need to see in it to know if it's worth a start?" | Send a one-page summary of their own numbers from this call, not a brochure. | "I'll send it today. Can we look at it together {day} for 10 minutes?" If no date, treat it as a no. |
| **"I need to talk to my partner / spouse"** | Real shared decision (common in family businesses), or a soft out | "Makes sense. What do you think they'll ask?" | Answer those questions now. Offer: "Want 10 minutes with both of you so you're not stuck relaying it?" | Set the joint time on this call. Never go around the partner. |
| **"Busy season, not now"** | Real overload, and also when the problem costs most | Label: "Sounds like you're slammed." Calibrated: "When it's this busy, what happens to the requests nobody gets to?" | "Your part is {their real effort}. We do the rest." Restate any guarantee from CONTEXT.md. | "Would you be against doing setup after hours one evening?" Or set a dated check-back. |
| **"Burned by agencies / vendors"** | Paid for nothing, locked in, couldn't get out, didn't own their stuff | Accusation audit: "You probably expect a long contract, a setup fee and a report full of numbers that don't mean anything." | State only what's true per CONTEXT.md (contract length, fees, who owns what, how to cancel). | "What happened last time?" Listen. Answer only that. |
| **"I'll think about it"** | One piece of certainty missing (price, whether it works, trust) | "Sure. What part are you still thinking through?" | Answer the one part they name. No new features. | "Would it be unreasonable to pencil in {day} for a yes or no?" |
| **Spanish preference** ("Prefiero en español") | Comfort, respect, and their customers' language | Switch if the caller can hold the call in Spanish. If not, say so honestly in simple Spanish and offer a written Spanish follow-up (WhatsApp only if they gave that number). | Be honest about what the project can and can't do in Spanish (per CONTEXT.md). Never promise a date you don't control. | See SPANISH-SELLING.md. |

Other objections (contract, proof, "is this AI", word of mouth, "we already use a tool") are in PLAYBOOK section 4. Don't repeat them here.

**What we never do on the call.** Discount below the live offer. Invent a deadline. Promise jobs, revenue or rankings. Name software or mechanisms in the pitch unless your copy rules allow it. Repeat a claim from your cold email that turned out to be wrong; correct it instead. Keep talking after a clear no.

## After the call (within the hour)

1. Write a 5-line recap in their words: current state, where they want to be, the gap number they agreed to, the decision, the dated next step.
2. Send the agreed next step (start checklist, summary, or calendar hold). Martha drafts, a human sends.
3. Record the outcome in your pipeline, with what they said, so LESSONS.md can learn from it.
